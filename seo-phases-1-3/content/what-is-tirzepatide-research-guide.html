Tirzepatide (development code LY3298176) is a synthetic, fatty-acid-modified peptide that activates both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR) and the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R). In the laboratory it is a reference compound for studying dual incretin-receptor agonism, receptor signalling bias and structure–activity relationships of multi-receptor peptides. This guide covers the compound's identity, the pharmacology described in peer-reviewed papers, and the documentation to review before using a lot.

Receptor pharmacology

Dual incretin-receptor agonism

In the discovery report, LY3298176 activated both GIP and GLP-1 receptor signalling in cell lines expressing recombinant or endogenous incretin receptors, and in mice it acted through both receptors in glucose-tolerance experiments [1]. The molecule was designed for once-weekly administration, so it is also studied as a long-acting peptide [1].

Structural basis and signalling bias

Cryo-electron microscopy and molecular-dynamics work showed that tirzepatide activates the GIPR in a way that closely resembles native GIP, but differs markedly from GLP-1 at the GLP-1R. The result is less agonist-induced receptor desensitisation [2]. The study shows how the fatty-acid modification and the amino-acid sequence together shape the behaviour of a multi-receptor agonist.

These points describe published receptor pharmacology used to design laboratory experiments. They are not claims about the effects of this research material.

Typical laboratory research questions

  • cAMP and β-arrestin signalling at GIPR versus GLP-1R in cell-based assays.
  • Receptor internalisation and desensitisation compared with selective GLP-1R agonists; see single vs dual agonist peptides.
  • Comparisons with triple agonists such as retatrutide, or with semaglutide.

Analytical identity and purity

For a lipidated peptide of about 4.8 kDa, the certificate of analysis should report identity by LC-MS (deconvoluted mass matching the expected value) and purity by reversed-phase HPLC. Lipidated peptides can show aggregation and closely eluting related substances, so check the chromatogram rather than only the headline percentage. Frequently asked questions

Why is tirzepatide called a dual agonist?

Because a single peptide activates two receptors, GIPR and GLP-1R [1].

How is it different from a triple agonist?

Triple agonists such as retatrutide also activate the glucagon receptor. See triple-agonist peptides.

Is this the same as an approved tirzepatide medicine?

No. Approved medicines are regulated drug products. OligoPoly tirzepatide is a research material for laboratory use only.
